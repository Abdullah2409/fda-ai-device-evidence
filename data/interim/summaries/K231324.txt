Device Description Summary 21 CFR 807.92(a)(4)
DASI Dimensions is an image post-processing software system intended for clinical decision support in the context of pre-procedural
planning ofTranscatheter Aortic Valve Replacement (TAVR) procedures. The software provides users with a report of generated
dimensions of cardiac structures. DASI Dimensions software is not operated by physicians.
DASI Dimensions is an off-site software system that receives cardiologists and/or radiologists' input via upload of patient multiphase
DICOM computed tomography angiography (CTA) chest image files through DASI Simulations web portal. The processed report is then
available for viewing and downloading. The report is generated using proprietary algorithms that (a) detect key aortic root control
points with the assistance of a static deep learning artificial intelligence (Al) model and (b) calculate anatomical measurements relevant
for pre-TAVR evaluation. DASI Simulations engineers perform quality checks at both steps before releasing the report to the end user via
the portal.UA:>I uImensIons aoes not come in contact wIrn rne patient, nor aoes It control any me sustaining aevIces. 1n e mrormauon provIaea oy
DASI Dimensions is not diagnostic, nor does it determine recommended medical care.
The cardiologists and/or radiologists receiving the results retains the responsibility for interpreting and validating all information and
making all patient treatment decisions.
DASI Dimensions:
• Supports quantification of cardiac structures for pre-procedural planning of aortic stenosis patients in consideration for potential TAVR
procedures.
• Provides the measurement of different structures of the heart, e.g., aorta, aortic root, aortic valve.

Indications for Use Comparison 21 CFR 807.92(a)(5)
DASI Dimensions and the predicate device have similar indications for use and have the same intended use. Both devices provide the
end-user with reports for pre-operative planning for cardiovascular interventions with images and measurements. The difference in the
two indications is the support of certain modules (dimensions in coronary arteries, quantification of calcifications and certain
visualization techniques such as volume rendering, MPR etc.) by the predicate device that are not supported by DASI Dimensions.
Technological Comparison 21 CFR 807.92(a)(6)
A comparison of the technological characteristics of the subject device to the predicate device is presented below:
1. Target Area: The predicate device's target areas include the heart, ascending, descending aorta and peripheral vasculature. The
subject device's target areas are cardiac structures specific to Transcatheter Aortic Valve Replacement such as aortic root, aortic valve,
sinus of valsalva, sinotubular junction and ascending aorta.
2. Functionality: Both the subject and predicate device enable linear (diameter/perimeter), angular and areal measurements of cardiac
structures with annotations. The predicate device has additional tools for volume measurements and scoring of calcification in addition
to the functionality described above.
3. Interpretation of Images: The outputs of both the subject and predicate devices are interpreted by Health Care Professionals (HCP).
4. Technical Method: Both the subject and predicate devices use an automated algorithm involving segmentation and centerline
detection of cardiac structures.
5. Software Architecture: Both the subject and predicate devices have multiphase DICOM data provided by clinical users as input, while
the reports are prepared by qualified operators and made available digitally.

As described in the DASI Dimensions Software Verification and Validation Plan (TPl 31 ), the following testing was completed:
(i) Software Verification and Validation: Software verification activities were done to verify that design inputs described in the
software requirements specifications can be traced to design outputs including corresponding software design documents, test
procedures, and system software. Software validation activities were performed to ensure that the design outputs meet design inputs
using a complete DASI Dimensions system software release.
a. Traceability Matrix (RMFl 12): Generated to trace each functional requirement (design input) through implementation
(design output). Functional Requirements testing (TRl 42 and TR143) were performed to ensure that each design input can be traced to
the design output. The results showed successful performance of the functional testing.
b. Al Control Point Detection Validation Study (WI 112 and TRl 32) trained and tested the Al model using training, validation,
and testing datasets. Control point deviation has been shown to have clinically significant impact on the final outputs when > 3 mm of
error is introduced thus an acceptance criteria of s; 3 mm was used to determine success of an individual control point. In the Al testing
dataset the Al generated control points were compared to manual control points generated by qualified DASI Dimensions operators
(ground truth), error resulted in a success rate of 75.3% of points. All testing, validation, and training cases were independent of each
other and were not used in any other validation studies.
c. Automatic Measurements Validation Study (TPl 33 and TRl 33) was performed to verify the accuracy of the automatic
measurement process of DASI Dimensions. The automatic measurement outputs generated using control points were compared to the
measurements generated manually by clinicians using current standard of care methods. The primary measurements showed s;l 5%
error in s;95% of cases thus satisfying the acceptance criteria. The secondary measurements showed s;20% error in s;95% of the cases
thus satisfying the acceptance criteria. The automatic measurement outputs showed agreement with the manual measurements
generated by clinicians with no statistically significant differences between the two measurement groups.
d. Performance Validation Study (TPl 34 and TRl 34) was used as validation of the overall performance of the device. A cohort
of patient CT images (n=40), representative of the general population of patients with tricuspid aortic valves and aortic stenosis, were
processed through the DASI Dimensions semi-automatic Al enhanced workflow by trained DASI Simulations operators where outputs
were compared to qualified clinician truther generated measurements. The Al generated control points performed at an 85.3% success
rate achieving the acceptance criteria of ~75%. The mean percentage error in primary outputs was 0.93% (Cl: positive 8.65%, negative
-6.80%) and -1.02% (Cl: positive 3.49%, negative -5.54%) respectively for the annulus area and perimeter, satisfying the acceptance
criteria of s;l 0% error in ~95% of cases. The mean percentage difference in secondary outputs was 4.77% (Cl: positive 11.26%, negative
-1.72%), 4.13% (Cl: positive 11.61%, negative -3.35%), 3.29% (Cl: 8.83%, negative -2.25%) respectively for the left-, right-and non
coronary sinus of valsalva diameters, 1.25% (Cl: positive 8.80%, negative -6.30%) and 2.200/4 (Cl: positive 8.46%, negative -4.06%)
respectively for sinotubular junction maximum and minimum diameters, and 0.12% (Cl: positive 5.29%, negative -5.05%) for the
ascending aorta maximum diameter, satisfying the acceptance criteria of s; 15% error in ~95% of cases. The mean percentage difference
in the tertiary output between DASI Dimensions and clinician truthing was 2.66% (Cl: positive 24.98%, negative -19.66%) for the aortic
valve angle, satisfying the acceptance criteria of s;25% error in ~95% of cases. No trends were observed between the aortic valve annulus
error and patient or scan characteristics. Scans were independent of those used for Al training or any other validation study.
e. Operator Variability Study (TPl 36 and TRl 36) validated the repeatability and reproducibility of DASI Dimensions outputs
produced by different operators. The study evaluated whether the observed inter-operator variability in the selection of control points
and alteration of measurement location/contours can introduce significant difference in the DASI Dimensions measurement outputs.
Using a dataset of CT As (n = 14), comparison of the annulus area outputs from DASI Dimensions made by trained DASI Simulations
operators (n = 5) showed excellent inter-operator agreement (precision) and to clinician ground truth measurements (accuracy), with an
ICC of 0.96 ands; 10% difference from clinician measurements in ~95% of cases. Both acceptance criteria were met, thus demonstrating
that the variability in DASI Dimensions outputs due to operator inputs is within acceptable limits.
